## 1. Purpose

Vendelligent Technologies LLC ("Vendelligent" or "the Company") is committed to a workplace where everyone is treated with dignity, consistent with the value **Respect by default**. This policy defines harassment, discrimination, bullying and retaliation, explains how to report them, and sets the grievance procedure, with timelines, that People & Culture follows.

## 2. Scope

This policy covers conduct that occurs in Riyadh, Dubai and the Remote Engineering Hub; at retail partner sites where Smart Machines are installed; at Company events and on business travel; and in Slack, email, video calls, social media and messaging apps when connected to work. It protects employees, contractors, interns, job applicants, and also consumers, retail partner staff and brand partner staff when they deal with Company people. It applies equally to conduct by a colleague, a manager, a Director or a third party.

## 3. Definitions

- **Harassment**: unwelcome conduct, one-off or repeated, that violates a person's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading or offensive environment. Intent is not required; the effect on the person and how a reasonable person would see it are what count.
- **Sexual harassment**: unwelcome conduct of a sexual nature, including comments, jokes, touching, messages, images, requests for sexual favours, or linking any benefit or detriment to sexual conduct.
- **Bullying**: repeated, unreasonable behaviour directed at a person that undermines, humiliates or intimidates them. It differs from legitimate management of performance carried out respectfully.
- **Discrimination**: less favourable treatment, or a rule or practice that disadvantages a person or group, because of a protected characteristic.
- **Retaliation**: any adverse treatment of a person because they raised or supported a concern, took part in an investigation, or used SpeakUp. Retaliation is zero tolerance.
- **Grievance**: a formal complaint by an employee about work, treatment, working conditions or relationships.
- **Complainant**: the person raising a concern. **Respondent**: the person the concern is about.
- **Working day**: a day on which the employee's Anchor Office is open.

## 4. Protected Characteristics

The Company prohibits discrimination on the basis of: sex or gender, pregnancy, maternity or family status, marital status, race, colour, nationality or national origin, ethnicity, religion or belief (including observance of Ramadan, prayer and Hajj), age, disability or health condition, and any other characteristic protected by the law of the country in which the person works. This applies to recruitment, pay, promotion (April and October cycles), training, benefits, discipline, termination and day-to-day treatment.

Reasonable adjustments will be considered for disabilities and health conditions, and for religious observance, where practicable.

## 5. Examples of Prohibited Conduct

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Harassment | Offensive jokes or nicknames; derogatory comments about appearance, accent or religion; shouting or humiliating a person in front of others; unwanted messages after hours; sharing offensive images in Slack |
| Sexual harassment | Unwanted touching; sexual comments or messages; repeated invitations after refusal; suggesting a promotion or contract renewal depends on sexual conduct |
| Bullying | Persistent undue criticism; excluding someone from meetings or information; unreasonable workloads set to punish; spreading rumours |
| Discrimination | Refusing a promotion because of pregnancy; paying differently for the same role because of nationality; rejecting a candidate because of age; mocking religious practice |
| Retaliation | Cutting a person's hours, ignoring them or blocking progression after they raised a concern; threatening a witness; trying to identify an anonymous reporter |
| Third-party conduct | A consumer or retail partner staff member harassing a technician or sales colleague at a site |

Conduct is covered even if it takes place outside working hours, away from the office, or on personal channels, if it affects the working relationship.

## 6. Reporting Channels

An employee who experiences or witnesses conduct covered by this policy may use any of these channels, and does not have to start with their manager.

| Channel | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Line manager | Verbally or in writing | The manager must log the matter in PeopleDesk **the same working day** and must not investigate it alone |
| PeopleDesk, "Grievance" category | VendHub / PeopleDesk | Confidential, visible only to People & Culture |
| PeopleDesk, "Harassment/Discrimination" category | VendHub / PeopleDesk | Automatically restricted, visible only to People & Culture; treated as P1 |
| **SpeakUp** | Anonymous web form | Protected from retaliation; anonymous reports can be followed up through the case reference provided |
| Head of People & Culture directly | In person or email | Used when the employee does not want to use the system |
| CEO | Direct | For matters involving a Director or People & Culture |

There is no time limit for raising a concern, but reporting promptly makes it easier to investigate. Reports made in good faith are protected even if not upheld.

## 7. The AI HR Assistant and These Matters

The AI HR Assistant is a Tier-0 channel. It may explain this policy, but it **must always route harassment, discrimination and grievance matters to a human P1 ticket** in PeopleDesk. It must not investigate, give a view on whether conduct is harassment, advise on outcomes, or close a case. When an employee describes such a situation, the assistant must explain the available channels (including SpeakUp and EAP), prepare a draft ticket if the employee agrees, and make clear that a member of People & Culture will respond within 4 business hours.

## 8. Grievance Procedure

### 8.1 Informal resolution

For minor matters, an employee may first try to resolve the issue directly or with the help of their manager or People & Culture. Informal resolution is optional and never required for harassment, discrimination or retaliation. It is not appropriate where there is a power imbalance or the complainant does not wish to use it.

### 8.2 Formal grievance: timelines

| Stage | Timeline |
|---|---|
| First response to a harassment or safety allegation (P1) | Within 4 business hours |
| Written acknowledgement of a grievance | Within **2 working days** of receipt |
| Investigation started (harassment, discrimination, safety P1) | Within 2 working days |
| Investigation outcome communicated | Within **15 working days** of receipt |
| Extension for complex cases | Up to **30 working days** in total, with written notice and reasons to the complainant before day 15 ends |
| Appeal | Within **5 working days** of the outcome |

### 8.3 Investigation steps

1. **Intake and triage.** People & Culture records the case in PeopleDesk, assigns priority (P1 for harassment, discrimination or safety), and notifies the Head of People & Culture.
2. **Acknowledgement.** The complainant receives written acknowledgement within 2 working days, with the case reference, the process and the expected timeline.
3. **Appointment of investigator.** The Head of People & Culture appoints an impartial investigator who has no conflict of interest. Legal & Compliance may be consulted.
4. **Interim measures** are considered (section 9).
5. **Notification of the respondent.** The respondent is told of the allegations in enough detail to respond, and reminded of the confidentiality and anti-retaliation rules.
6. **Evidence gathering.** The investigator interviews the complainant, respondent and witnesses, and reviews documents, messages, system logs and site records. Each interviewee may be accompanied by a colleague.
7. **Findings.** The investigator decides, on the balance of probabilities, whether the allegation is substantiated, partly substantiated or not substantiated, and writes a report.
8. **Decision and outcome.** The Head of People & Culture (or a Director not involved) decides the outcome. The complainant and respondent are told the outcome in writing within 15 working days, or within the extended period. Where misconduct is found, the matter is referred to VND-HR-011 Disciplinary Policy and Procedure.
9. **Follow-up.** People & Culture checks in with the complainant at 30 days and again at 90 days to confirm that there has been no retaliation and that the outcome is working.

## 9. Interim Measures

While an investigation is open, People & Culture may, without finding fault, put in place measures to protect everyone involved. These may include changing reporting lines or seating, adjusting schedules or Anchor Office days, moving a site assignment, restricting contact, temporary remote working, paid leave, or temporary suspension of access to specific channels. Measures must not disadvantage the complainant. The complainant will be consulted before any change affecting them. Safety threats are also escalated to the COO and local emergency services.

## 10. Confidentiality

Cases are handled on a need-to-know basis. PeopleDesk records in the restricted categories are visible only to People & Culture. Everyone involved must keep the matter confidential and not discuss it on Slack, social media or with colleagues beyond their support person. Confidentiality cannot be absolute: information may be shared with the investigator, the decision maker, Legal & Compliance and authorities where required by law. Breach of confidentiality may lead to disciplinary action. Anonymous SpeakUp reports are handled without trying to identify the reporter.

## 11. Appeal

A complainant or respondent who disagrees with the outcome may appeal in writing through PeopleDesk **within 5 working days** of receiving the outcome. The appeal is heard by a Director who was not previously involved in the case. If a Director was involved in the case, the appeal is heard by the CEO. The appeal reviews whether the procedure was followed fairly, whether the outcome was reasonable and whether new evidence exists. The appeal decision is final within the Company and is communicated in writing. Appeals about disciplinary action follow VND-HR-011.

## 12. Matters Involving a Director

If the respondent is a Director (L5), the Head of People & Culture escalates the case immediately to the **CEO**, who appoints an investigator independent of the Director's department, which may be external. The Director does not take part in selecting the investigator, has no access to the case file, and does not supervise or evaluate the complainant for the duration of the case. If the respondent is the Head of People & Culture or the CEO, the matter is raised with the CEO or the Company's board respectively, with Legal & Compliance support.

## 13. Support for Employees

- **Employee Assistance Programme (EAP):** confidential counselling with **6 free sessions per year**, available to all employment types including interns. Employees can use it before, during or after a case.
- A support person (a colleague or trusted person) may accompany an employee to meetings.
- People & Culture will check in with the complainant and respondent at each stage.
- Reasonable adjustments to work arrangements are available while a case is open.
- Sick leave certificates for stress-related conditions are treated as confidential (see VND-HR-004).

## 14. Retaliation Protection

Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith, supports another person, takes part in an investigation or uses SpeakUp is prohibited and is gross misconduct that can lead to dismissal. A person who believes they are experiencing retaliation must report it via PeopleDesk "Harassment/Discrimination" or SpeakUp. It will be handled as a new P1 case. Knowingly false allegations made in bad faith are also misconduct, but a concern that is not upheld is not, on its own, evidence of bad faith.

## 15. Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I have to tell my manager first?** No. You can use PeopleDesk, SpeakUp or go directly to the Head of People & Culture.

**How quickly will my grievance be acknowledged?** Within 2 working days. Harassment or safety allegations receive a first response within 4 business hours.

**How long does an investigation take?** The outcome is due within 15 working days, extendable to 30 working days for complex cases, with written notice.

**Can I appeal?** Yes, within 5 working days of the outcome, to a Director not previously involved (or to the CEO if a Director was involved).

**What if the person I am complaining about is a Director?** The case goes to the CEO, who appoints an independent investigator.

**Can I ask the AI HR Assistant to handle my harassment complaint?** The assistant can explain your options but must route the matter to a human P1 ticket. It cannot investigate or decide.

**Is counselling available?** Yes, the EAP gives 6 free confidential sessions per year to all employment types.

## 16. Roles and Responsibilities

- **Employees**: treat others with respect, report concerns, cooperate with investigations and keep cases confidential.
- **Line managers**: log reports in PeopleDesk the same working day, prevent retaliation and do not investigate alone.
- **People & Culture**: triages, investigates, manages timelines, supports employees and tracks follow-up.
- **Head of People & Culture**: appoints investigators, decides outcomes and escalates to the CEO.
- **Directors**: ensure their teams follow the policy and recuse themselves from cases involving them.
- **CEO**: handles cases involving a Director and Director-level appeals; approves this policy.
- **COO**: informed of safety threats.
- **AI HR Assistant**: routes these matters to a human P1 ticket.

## 17. Exceptions and Escalation

No exceptions apply to the prohibition on harassment, discrimination and retaliation. Timelines may be extended only as stated in section 8.2. P1 matters always escalate to the Head of People & Culture, and to the CEO where a Director is involved. Safety threats also escalate to the COO and local emergency services. Procedural details are in VND-SOP-104.
